HEXAGRAM LABEL: A
PRIMARY: 36 地火明夷 -> CHANGED: 11 地天泰 (Line 2)
MUTUAL: 雷水解

1. SITUATION DESCRIPTION
Earth above fire: the sun has gone below the horizon. Ming Yi is the hexagram of brightness wounded — light that still burns but must burn concealed. The judgment counsels "利艱貞": it is favourable to remain correct through hardship. The use trigram Li (fire) feeds the body trigram Kun (earth) — the situation actively nourishes the subject, but does so by requiring the subject to accept obscurity.

2. KEY ACTORS / FORCES
- Kun as body: receptive, broad, absorbing. It does not contest; it holds ground and takes what comes.
- Li as use: brilliance, display, the thing everyone looks at. It is being covered over.
- The second line is central and correct but has no answering line above. Its text: "明夷，夷于左股，用拯馬壯，吉" — wounded in the left thigh, rescued by the strength of a horse; auspicious. The injury is real but not to the vital organs, and rescue comes through raw power rather than cleverness.

3. PROCESS NARRATIVE
Something bright is struck early and limps. The wound is to the left thigh — mobility is impaired, not life. What saves the situation is not subtlety but a strong horse: sheer momentum applied at the right moment. The mutual hexagram Jie (解, Deliverance) sits in the middle of the arc — a knot loosening, tension released after being held. The transformation into Tai (泰, Peace) is the largest possible reversal in the book: heaven and earth exchanging places, obstruction becoming free circulation. What began in eclipse ends in open communication.

4. QUALITATIVE OUTCOME CHARACTER
Damage sustained early, converted into a broadly favourable settlement. The outcome is not brilliant — brilliance is precisely what was suppressed — but it is solid, wide, and shared. The final taste is of things falling into their proper places after a period where nothing was visible.

5. SPECIFIC IMAGERY / METAPHORS
- The sun sinking into the earth: a light that continues underground.
- A wound to the left thigh: hobbled, still standing.
- A strong horse pulling the injured clear: rescue by power, not finesse.
- Heaven and earth changing seats: a total reordering of who is above and who below.

6. WARNINGS / SURPRISES
The surprise is the size of the reversal. Ming Yi is one of the darkest images in the sequence, yet the single moving line converts it into Peace. The warning attached: the second line has no correspondent above, so no help arrives from authority. Whatever rescue comes must be self-generated. Concealment is the strategy, not the failure — the subject who insists on being seen during Ming Yi is the one who does not survive to reach Tai.
