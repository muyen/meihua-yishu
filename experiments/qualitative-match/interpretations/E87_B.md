This is the hexagram of erosion from underneath. Body and use are both earth-natured, in 比和 — harmony without tension, but also without any generative push.

The arc is a structured, staged removal — not a collapse but a dismantling that proceeds in order from the bottom. The mutual hexagram is Kun (坤) doubled — pure receptivity, no resistance anywhere in the middle of the situation. The transformation into Meng (蒙, Youthful Folly) — water under the mountain — introduces the note of not-yet-knowing: a spring at the foot of a hill that has not found its channel, inexperience meeting a situation it does not understand.

4. QUALITATIVE OUTCOME CHARACTER
Progressive loss of support, unresisted, ending in confusion rather than catastrophe. There is no dramatic break here; things are taken away in order, and what remains does not understand its own position. The taste is of steady subtraction.

5. SPECIFIC IMAGERY / METAPHORS
- A bed dismantled upward from the legs: destruction that proceeds methodically toward the sleeper.

The one genuine surprise is the transformation: Splitting Apart resolves into Youthful Folly, which is a hexagram of teaching and beginnings. What is stripped away leaves behind something that has to learn from scratch.
