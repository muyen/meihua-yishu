EVENT: Michigan gubernatorial primaries
HEXAGRAM LABEL: B
PRIMARY: 5 水天需 -> CHANGED: 43 澤天夬 (Line 4)
MUTUAL: 火澤睽

1. SITUATION DESCRIPTION
Water above heaven: Xu, Waiting. Clouds risen into the sky with the rain not yet fallen — the hexagram of necessary delay. The judgment is confident about the eventual crossing: "有孚，光亨，貞吉，利涉大川". The body trigram drains into the use trigram: the subject expends itself into the situation.

2. KEY ACTORS / FORCES
- Kan above: water, danger suspended overhead.
- Qian below: heaven, the creative force that must nevertheless wait.
- The fourth line is correct in place with a correspondent, but rests on a firm line — soft over hard, structurally unstable. Its text: "需于血，出自穴" — waiting in blood; coming out of the pit. This is the darkest line of the hexagram, and it is also the one where the position escapes.

3. PROCESS NARRATIVE
The Xu sequence moves the waiting closer to danger step by step — the outskirts, the sand, the mud, and then blood. The fourth line is the point of contact: the waiting is now happening inside injury. But the second half of the line is movement outward — 出自穴, emerging from the hole. Having reached the worst position, the figure gets out of it. The mutual hexagram Kui (睽, Opposition) marks divergence at the centre. The transformation into Guai (夬, Breakthrough) — five firm lines pushing out one soft one at the top — converts the escape into a decisive clearing: the obstruction is removed rather than avoided.

4. QUALITATIVE OUTCOME CHARACTER
A wait that becomes costly and then breaks decisively in the subject's favour. The flavour is of the worst part being survived rather than avoided, and of the resolution arriving as a clean removal of what was in the way. Damage first, then breakthrough.

5. SPECIFIC IMAGERY / METAPHORS
- Clouds full and holding: everything ready, nothing yet released.
- Waiting in blood: the delay carried on into actual injury.
- Climbing out of the pit: escape from the position of maximum exposure.
- Breakthrough: the last obstruction pushed off the top.

6. WARNINGS / SURPRISES
The line is unusual in containing both the worst image and the escape in a single sentence, with no verdict attached to either. The structural instability of the position suggests the escape is not comfortable. The surprise is the transformation into Guai: waiting, the most passive hexagram in the book, resolving into the most decisive one. Whatever is endured here ends abruptly rather than fading out.
