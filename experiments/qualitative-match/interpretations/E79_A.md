1. SITUATION DESCRIPTION
Thunder above fire: Feng, Abundance, read at its opening line. Movement and brightness together — the greatest expansion in the sequence, and the hexagram that warns about noon precisely because noon is the beginning of decline. The body trigram drains into the use trigram: the subject expends itself.

2. KEY ACTORS / FORCES
- Zhen above: thunder, motion, noise.
- Li below: fire, clarity, illumination.
- The first line is correct in place but has no answering line, and carries a yielding line above it. Its text: "遇其配主，雖旬无咎，往有尚" — he meets his destined counterpart; though they are equals for ten days there is no blame; going forward brings esteem. An encounter between matched parties, an interval where neither dominates, and then movement that is rewarded.

3. PROCESS NARRATIVE
The arc opens with a meeting of equals — 配主 is the paired host, the counterpart of the same rank. The text specifically licenses a period of parity: 旬, a ten-day cycle, during which sameness of standing is not a problem. Then the advice turns to motion: going forward earns regard. The mutual hexagram Da Guo (大過) is the bending ridgepole, structural strain at the centre. The transformation into Xiao Guo (小過, Small Exceeding) — thunder over mountain — reduces the scale: the bird's call that should stay low, the small things done well rather than the large thing attempted.

4. QUALITATIVE OUTCOME CHARACTER
An even meeting followed by a rewarded advance, resolving into something smaller than it started. The flavour is of two well-matched parties, a period where neither pulls ahead, then a move that gains credit — but the frame contracts. What ends is more modest than what began.

5. SPECIFIC IMAGERY / METAPHORS
- Meeting one's counterpart: the encounter with someone of exactly one's own standing.
- Ten days of parity: an interval where equality is explicitly not a fault.
- The ridgepole under strain: load concentrated in the middle.
- The bird that should not fly high: the counsel to keep the scale small.

6. WARNINGS / SURPRISES
The first line of Feng is unusually permissive — it grants an exception for a bounded period, which most line texts do not do. The warning is in the transformation: Abundance becomes Small Exceeding, so the resolution is a contraction of ambit. Whatever advance is made here, the closing register is modest rather than expansive.
