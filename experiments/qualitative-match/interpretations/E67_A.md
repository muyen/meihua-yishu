EVENT: Commonwealth Games track cycling at the Sir Chris Hoy Velodrome
HEXAGRAM LABEL: A
PRIMARY: 59 風水渙 -> CHANGED: 6 天水訟 (Line 4)
MUTUAL: 山雷頤

1. SITUATION DESCRIPTION
Wind above water: Huan, Dispersion. Wind moving across water and breaking it up — dissolution of what has hardened, and the loosening of rigid attachments. The judgment favours crossing the great water. The body trigram drains into the use trigram: the subject expends itself.

2. KEY ACTORS / FORCES
- Xun above: wind, the agent of scattering.
- Kan below: water, danger and depth.
- The fourth line is correct in place but has no answering line. Its text: "渙其群，元吉。渙有丘，匪夷所思" — he disperses his group; supreme good fortune. In the dispersion there is a mound; this is not what ordinary thinking would arrive at.

3. PROCESS NARRATIVE
The arc concerns breaking things up, and at the fourth line what is broken up is one's own faction. Dissolving the group one belongs to earns 元吉 — supreme good fortune, the strongest verdict in the vocabulary. Then the second image: out of the scattering, a hill accumulates. The dispersal at one level produces a gathering at another. The line closes with 匪夷所思, an explicit statement that this is counterintuitive — the book flagging its own logic as non-obvious. The mutual hexagram Yi (頤, Nourishment) sits at the centre. The transformation into Song (訟, Conflict) introduces dispute: heaven and water moving apart.

4. QUALITATIVE OUTCOME CHARACTER
A faction dissolved to great advantage, producing an unexpected accumulation, resolving into contention. The flavour is of breaking up a smaller loyalty and finding something larger forms as a result, followed by a dispute over what has formed.

5. SPECIFIC IMAGERY / METAPHORS
- Wind scattering a surface that looked solid.
- Dissolving one's own group: the partisan tie deliberately released.
- A mound rising out of the scattering: accumulation as a product of dispersal.
- "Not what ordinary thinking would arrive at": the text marking its own counterintuitiveness.

6. WARNINGS / SURPRISES
元吉 appears rarely and this is one of its clearest instances, attached to an act that looks like self-harm. The line has no correspondent, so the dissolution is unilateral. The surprise is the transformation into Conflict: the supremely favourable act produces something worth arguing over. Expect a breakup that yields an unexpected consolidation, and then friction about it.
