EVENT: Geomagnetic storm window
HEXAGRAM LABEL: A
PRIMARY: 59 風水渙 -> CHANGED: 57 巽為風 (Line 3)
MUTUAL: 山雷頤

1. SITUATION DESCRIPTION
Wind above water: Huan, Dispersion. Wind moving over the surface of water, breaking it up — the hexagram of dissolution, of things scattering and of rigidities being loosened. The judgment includes "利涉大川", it is favourable to cross the great water. The use trigram feeds the body trigram: the situation nourishes the subject.

2. KEY ACTORS / FORCES
- Xun as body: wind, penetration, the dispersing agent.
- Kan as use: water, danger, the depth being crossed.
- The third line is incorrect in place, has a correspondent, and rests on a firm line — the unstable soft-over-hard arrangement. Its text: "渙其躬，无悔" — he disperses his own person; no regret. The dissolution is turned inward, applied to the self rather than to circumstances.

3. PROCESS NARRATIVE
The arc is one of breaking up what has congealed. At the third line the dispersal is reflexive — the subject dissolves their own position, gives up the fixed thing they were holding. The line is unusually clean: no blame, no warning, simply 无悔. The mutual hexagram Yi (頤, Nourishment) sits at the centre, the open jaws — what sustains, what is taken in. The transformation into Xun (巽) doubled — wind above wind — completes the direction: pure penetration, gentle repeated influence, nothing solid left anywhere in the figure. Dispersal becomes total.

4. QUALITATIVE OUTCOME CHARACTER
A breaking-up that is chosen rather than suffered, ending in a state with no fixed structure at all. The flavour is of letting go of one's own position and finding it costs nothing, followed by a condition of pure flow. Nothing is held; nothing is lost either.

5. SPECIFIC IMAGERY / METAPHORS
- Wind ruffling water: a solid-looking surface broken into pieces.
- Dispersing one's own body: dissolution turned on the self, willingly.
- The open jaws at the centre: the question of what is taken in while everything else scatters.
- Wind doubled: influence that works only by repetition, never by force.

6. WARNINGS / SURPRISES
Huan is one of the few hexagrams where dissolution is the favourable act rather than the loss — the crossing of the great water is recommended, not warned against. The surprise is how little is charged for it: a line about giving up one's own standing carries no regret at all. The instability of the third position suggests the giving-up was not entirely voluntary in its timing.
